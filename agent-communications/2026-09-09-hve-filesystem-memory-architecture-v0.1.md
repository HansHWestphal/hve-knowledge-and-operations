## 1. Executive position

Hans's question: should HVE adopt this white paper's filesystem-based memory pattern as the long-term memory architecture for hve-life-os, decided by team agent consensus. This brief's position: **adopt it as a reference architecture — not as an immediate migration** — and run agent consensus around the three decisions where the paper shows the risk actually concentrates:

1. **Management-agent investment.** The paper's most reproducible negative finding is that organization *erodes* over time for every management agent except the strongest model tested (RQ4; Figure 9: contract-adherence drifts as stores grow, only the strongest curator holds it roughly constant). If HVE's memory steward is a mid-tier model, the paper says the organization will not survive contact with growth.
2. **Tooling, not just the model, is the architecture lever.** "Changing the tool set alone reshapes the store as strongly as swapping the model" (Abstract; §4.5). The shell-harness chain was the best chain the weakest tested curator produced (82.9%, net +11, p≈0.04 vs. its default-tools baseline; Table 8) — and the same paper finds adding a tool changes behavior without changing outcomes, while replacing the tool set changes outcomes. For HVE agents running on constrained local models with a fixed approved tool inventory, the tool/harness is the design knob the team can actually turn.
3. **The value case is search economy and graceful degradation, not answer quality.** Organization ≈ halved per-query search cost on dense material; the curated skills+mem (GS) store is cheapest per solved task at both model tiers and the only memory form that *degrades gracefully* when the executor is weak (GS +14 pts vs. episode log at gpt-4.1-mini, p≈0.02; the episode log only leads at the strong tier, +7, p≈0.23). No measured agent converted organization itself into better answers.

HVE already runs a shape close to the paper's three-role model (Librarian as management/curator; knowledge-library retrieval as search; task profiles as execution). That matters: the recommendation is not "build new", it is **name the roles, borrow the contracts, and let consensus turn on management strength and tooling before any migration.**

## 2. Source findings (what the paper actually establishes)

All items below are [S] source facts from the verified record; numbers as reported in the paper.

**Formal model (Abstract, §1).** Three roles around one memory filesystem: a *management agent* integrates and organizes incoming content; a *search agent* answers with cited sources; an *execution agent* supplies trajectories distilled into skills — one store serving declarative memory and procedural skills. The paper is, in its own words, the first systematic exploration of the field's dominant default: a directory tree of markdown files the agent reads, writes, and reorganizes through generic file tools — a medium prior systems skipped.

**Main results:**
- **Organization reliably buys search economy, most where material is dense.** On the two largest PersonaMem tiers (137–612 KB verbatim), reorganized/curated stores cut per-query search price by half or more (1.4¢ vs. 4.0¢ at 32k; 1.6¢ vs. 3.9¢ at 128k); on the smaller conversational benchmarks, parity (2.1–2.5¢) (§4.2, Tables 1–2). Mechanism: structured stores spend *more* rounds but *far fewer* tokens per targeted read — structure trades effort for precision and wins only where the alternative scan is expensive.
- **No shape wins everywhere on quality.** Foldered sessions (simple, cheap structure) ties or tops three of four conversational benchmarks; the agent-curated store is the *weakest* on PersonaMem 32k (37.5 vs. verbatim dump's 78.1); chunk retrieval beats every curated store at 32k (71.9). One reorganizer flips sign across benchmarks — condensing *halved* REALTALK correctness (77.6 → 41.2) while helping PersonaMem 32k (68.8 vs. 56.2) (§4.2). The paper's own caveat: tiers rest on 32–158 questions and a ±1.3pt re-judge moves small-tier rankings, so per-tier orderings are suggestive, not law.
- **Payoff depends on who consumes the memory.** Strong executor: verbatim episode log leads (87.1%). Weak executor: curated skills+mem (GS) leads by 10 points (76.4 vs. 66.4; net +14, p≈0.02) (Table 5). Mechanism: raw episode transcripts *demand* a strong executor — episode-log invalid-action rate jumps 19%→36% at the weak tier, GS only 12%→22% — while task-written guidance degrades gracefully. Memory is worth the most to the weaker executor (+23.5pt best lift), exactly where deployment is cheapest.
- **Stores become more useful as they grow; curated stores mature.** In the 140-task full-chain protocol, every store variant rises from first to last stretch; nothing degrades within 140 tasks; the weak executor's episode-log chain climbs from its no-store floor (51.4) to above the strong tier's average (91.4). The strongest curator's store essentially stops growing by task 35 (14 of 16 files exist by then) and is edited/merged thereafter, with per-task retrieval flat and cheapest on the board. Early memories survive overwhelmingly (deletion/rewrite rare); stronger curation shows as in-place editing, not replacement (Figure 8). The episode log, by contrast, grows unbounded (140 files, 270 KB at chain's end, retrieval cost climbing with store size: $7.88 vs. GS $2.97; Figure 7).
- **Taxonomy adherence is the property that erodes.** Distance-mirrors-relatedness adherence drifts as stores grow for every curator except the strongest model (Figure 9 right). The store can remain useful while its organization quietly degrades — the paper's own caveat on its positive story.
- **Curation is not compression, and is not free.** One store ends 35% *larger* than its input stream (curation as elaboration); another compresses to a quarter. Build effort per chunk stays flat — no economy of scale. Curation bills $7–11 per cell vs. zero for the mechanical log; yet GS remains cheapest per solved task at both tiers (5.7¢ at mini vs. 6.1¢ no-memory) (Figure 5; Tables 16–18).
- **The harness is a lever, not a wrapper.** Adding ranked whole-file search changed behavior without changing outcomes (tie, p≈0.85); replacing the tool set with a shell changed outcomes for the weakest curator (82.9%, its best chain). The paper concludes the harness "becomes a natural knob for inducing a target structure" (§4.5–4.7).

**What the paper does not establish (limits the team should weigh):** chains of ≤140 tasks and ≤128k streams; the paper itself says it cannot tell whether the large-stream premium is repaid on longer horizons. Benchmark question tiers (32–158 items) with deltas inside re-judge noise. One vendor's model families and rate card. Procedural/embodied skill-setting tasks, conversational benchmarks — not lived-life personal memory measured over months. This paper measures cost and quality over *weeks of simulated accumulation*; multi-month, low-throughput, human-privacy-bearing accumulation (the HVE profile) is untested by it. AI assistants used only for schematic figures; all reported data are measured.

## 3. HVE implications (Librarian interpretation)

[Labeled [I] unless otherwise marked. HVE system descriptions are from the current approved tool inventory and profile design — not from the paper.]

- **[I] HVE already runs the paper's three-role architecture, unnamed.** Librarian = management agent (capture → archive → process → curate; skill maintenance is exactly "execution trajectories distilled into skills"). The hve-knowledge-library retrieval path (status/documents/query/chunks — provenance-checked, bounded pages) = search agent. Task profiles (hve-life-os, collector, etc.) = execution agents producing trajectories. The paper's value for HVE is therefore not a new system — it is a vocabulary of failure modes, measurable contracts, and evidence for where our current choices already pay.
- **[I] Our strongest evidence-aligned choices are already in place.** (1) *Provenance plus validation as store-health instruments* — HVE's manifest, sha256, and validation pipeline operationalize the paper's central warning that *adherence to the store's own organizational contract is the property that erodes*; the paper's strongest curator held it constant, and HVE's structural answer for weaker stewards is instrumentation — failures log, manifest_reconciliation, validation warnings. (2) *Bounded retrieval* (max_chunks, max_chars, paginated chunks) is the paper's search-economy mechanism in production: fewer tokens per targeted read. (3) *Skill files as distilled procedures* — the skill_manage patch discipline (lessons, not logs; references named by topic) is the GS form, and the paper's clearest finding is that this form is the one that survives when the executor is not the strongest model — exactly HVE's local-first, mixed-model reality. (4) *Reversible, edit-in-place curation* ("reversible curation over silent rewriting", conflicts routed to a human) matches the paper's observation that healthy stores are edited, not rewritten, and that early entries survive.
- **[I] Where HVE is exposed, per the paper:** (a) *Management-agent strength.* HVE's curation runs on local-first, mid-tier, rate-limited models — the profile the paper says loses organization over growth. Mitigation need not be a bigger model: the paper's harness lever (tooling, target-structure prompts) is available as prompt/tool design, and our validation stack is the detection. (b) *Episodic accumulation.* Appending raw session logs or intake dumps without curation is the paper's one unbounded, cost-climbing form — 140 files, 270 KB, retrieval price climbing with store size, serve-everything liability scaling with the store. (c) *Tool-inventory drift.* Because tool-set choice reshapes the store as strongly as model choice, HVE's approved-tool boundaries are *architectural decisions*, not convenience settings — a tool change without a store-shape review is an architecture change wearing a config costume.
- **[P] Proposal for consensus, not adoption:** (1) Adopt the three-role vocabulary as HVE's working definition of its memory architecture (Librarian=management, library-retrieval=search, task profiles=execution), with this paper as the *reference architecture* and its contracts as acceptance criteria for memory changes. (2) Make the paper's measurable contracts HVE's standing validation set: store stays useful as it grows; per-use cost stays flat or falls; early entries survive; taxonomy adherence is *measured* (HVE's instrument: manifest validation, failures, reconciliation on every curation pass). (3) Require a store-shape review note before any approved-tool change touching memory. (4) Keep the current markdown-file + approved-tool + validation stack — the paper shows no reason to change it and positive evidence that the curated/skills form we use is the strongest under weak executors.
- **[A] Assumptions to test, not assume:** that HVE's months-scale lived-life accumulation behaves like the paper's week-scale chains; that LoCoMo/REALTALK/PersonaMem question styles approximate HVE's real queries; that the paper's billing (one vendor's rate card) represents our token economics — HVE's transferable claim is the *shape* of the GS-vs-episode-log cost inversion, not the dollar figures.

## 4. Risks, controls, and objections

- **Risk: adopting the conclusion, skipping the evidence.** The team could treat one white paper as a mandate to migrate. *Control:* the brief's recommendation is reference architecture + vocabulary + validation contracts + a bounded pilot — not migration, new tooling, or policy. Any stronger step is a separate decision requiring Hans's explicit sign-off per the governance chain.
- **Risk: management-agent erosion is the most likely failure, and it is quiet.** The paper's eroding curators still *looked* organized while their contract drifted. HVE analog: the manifest stays valid while taxonomy (category naming, reference discipline, patch hygiene) rots, and retrieval degrades before validation flags it. *Control (formalize existing stack):* run manifest_reconciliation + validate + failures as a standing post-curation pass on any memory change; treat "validation passed" as necessary, not sufficient, for the four contracts.
- **Risk: tool-inventory drift as silent architecture change.** *Control:* add a store-shape impact note to the curation record on any memory-adjacent tool change — direct application of the harness-as-lever finding.
- **Risk: episode-log accumulation in HVE profiles.** Raw appends of session history or intake dumps are the paper's unbounded form. *Control (name existing stack):* no raw append into the durable library outside the capture → process → index pipeline; the failures log is where append-only mistakes surface.
- **Risk: over-trusting one paper for a months-scale life.** [Q] The paper's horizons (≤140 tasks, ≤128k) are short by HVE standards. *Control:* treat every [S] claim as conditional on HVE replicating the contract checks on HVE data — which is why the §5 pilot measures HVE sessions, not paper numbers.
- **Objection: "We already do this — why brief it?"** Because the paper (a) gives the team a shared vocabulary (three roles; GS vs. episode log; harness as lever) so debate happens about the same objects; (b) names one property we do not currently *measure* (taxonomy/contract adherence over time); (c) is the strongest published evidence that the form we already chose is the strongest under weak executors — letting consensus lean on evidence rather than preference.
- **Objection: "The numbers don't match our model mix."** Agreed — only the *inversions* transfer (GS > episode-log at weak tiers; retrieval-cost halving on dense material; maturity = editing not growth; harness as lever). The pilot re-derives the shape on HVE data.
- **Objection: "This is an architecture decision, and Hans is reserved."** Correct — the brief says so. What is asked of the team is consensus on *reference-architecture status, contract gates, tool-change gating, and pilot scope* — none of which is a migration, tooling, schema, or policy change.

## 5. Consensus questions and recommendation

**Decisions the team must make, in order:**
1. **Reference-architecture status** — adopt the three-role model as HVE's *named* working architecture for hve-life-os memory, with the paper as reference document, no migration? *Librarian position: yes — zero-cost, vocabulary-aligning, fully consistent with the approved stack.*
2. **Contract gates** — adopt the four growth contracts (usefulness grows; per-use cost flat or falls; early entries survive; adherence measured) as the standing acceptance criteria for any memory change? *Position: yes — the single highest-leverage change available without new tooling, because it is the detection mechanism for the paper's #1 observed failure mode.*
3. **Tool-change gating** — require a store-shape impact note for any memory-adjacent approved-tool change? *Position: yes — low cost, reversible, directly from the paper's strongest non-obvious finding.*
4. **Pilot scope** — a bounded, reversible, local-first pilot (≈4–8 HVE sessions over 2–3 weeks) measuring: per-query retrieval cost and rounds vs. current stack; reconciliation/validation warnings before and after curation passes; early-entry survival on a fixed seed; taxonomy-adherence on the curation prompts? *Position: yes — it is the HVE-specific evidence the paper cannot give us, and it is designed to fail cleanly.*
5. **Explicitly not decided here** — no new retrieval engine, no new store schema, no new tools, no migration, no policy change, no external publication. All are separate, owned, Hans-signed decisions, with pilot evidence as input.

**Recommendation [P]:** Adopt the three-role model as the named reference architecture for hve-life-os memory; adopt the four growth contracts as the standing acceptance gate for memory changes; gate memory-adjacent tool changes with a store-shape note; approve the bounded reversible pilot; and hold every further step to Hans on pilot evidence, not this brief. The paper's clearest finding supports HVE's current direction: the curated, skill-flavored, edit-in-place form is the one that survives when the model is not the strongest — and HVE runs on exactly such a model mix. Consensus on the architecture is not adoption of the conclusion; this brief keeps the two apart and asks the team to keep them apart.
